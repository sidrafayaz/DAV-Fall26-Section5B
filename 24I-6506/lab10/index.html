🔍 Key Analytical Finding

Unlike categorical tables where row sequence can be rearranged without penalty, time-series order is the information. While the dataset nominally spans the full calendar year from Jan 1 to Dec 31, there is an acute 9-day telemetry blackout in late winter (March 12–20). Any naive visualization using categorical axes or unbroken lines would falsely conceal this hardware failure.

⚠️ The Flaw of Monthly Averages

7. Day-of-Week vs. Monthly Drivers

Chart: Task 6

Is there a day-of-week pattern, or is the pattern mostly by month? What does that suggest about the cause of the pollution?
The variation is overwhelmingly monthly and seasonal (27.1 µg/m³ in July to 141.9 µg/m³ in Feb; spread of 114.8 µg/m³). Across days of the week, variation is marginal (weekdays average 88.3 µg/m³ vs weekends 81.4 µg/m³; spread of only 9.8 µg/m³). This proves that local vehicular traffic alone is not the primary determinant; macroscopic meteorological stagnation and transboundary/regional biomass burning dominate.

9. Trend Integrity & Sensor Diagnostics

Chart: Task 8

Is there a long-term rise or fall, or only a seasonal cycle? How many days are missing, and why is an unbroken line over the gap misleading?
The dataset reveals an annual recurrent seasonal cycle (U-shaped) rather than a monotonic multi-year trend. Exactly 9 days are missing (March 12–20). Connecting March 11 to March 21 with an unbroken line is scientifically invalid and misleading because it fabricates false continuity and falsely assumes air quality remained benign during an unmonitored equipment failure.

11. Scientific Critique of the Committee's Pie Chart

Visual Theory Reference

The committee member used a pie chart of monthly averages. List two things it could not tell the Director that your charts do.
1. Destruction of Time & Sequence: A pie chart displays static proportions with zero continuity, destroying the temporal order and obscuring the rate of change and cumulative risk (Tasks 2 & 3).
2. Complete Elimination of Outliers & Extremes: Averaging months into 12 slices erased the lethal spikes of 320.0 µg/m³ (Nov 8) and 245.0 µg/m³ (May 18) and hid the spread (Task 7).
3. Concealment of Hardware Outages: A pie chart gives March an equal slice, hiding that 9 days of vital telemetry were completely lost (Tasks 1 & 8).

🎯 12. Strategic Policy Recommendation for Outdoor School Activity Restrictions

Question: If the committee can restrict outdoor school activities for only two months, which two would you pick? Support your answer with evidence from at least two different charts.

Primary Strategic Recommendation: November and December

  • Evidence 1 (Task 2 & 7 - Peak Hazard & Dispersion): November experienced the single highest pollution peak of the entire year (320.0 µg/m³ on Nov 8) and the widest volatility spread (239.1 µg/m³). December recorded 11 Severe smog days (> 150 µg/m³) with an average of 138.8 µg/m³.
  • Evidence 2 (Task 3 & 5 - Total Population Exposure): In both November and December, 100% of recorded days breached the 75 µg/m³ safety ceiling (30/30 days in Nov, 31/31 days in Dec). Together they contributed 61 consecutive days of hazardous air.
  • Evidence 3 (Task 5 - Severe Day Concentration): December has the highest frequency of Severe air days in the year (11 days), followed by November with 5 days, exposing developing children's lungs to critical respiratory risks during active school terms.
  • Alternative Consideration: While January and February also have high monthly means (137.6 and 141.9 µg/m³), winter break already curtails school exposure in January. Imposing restrictions during November and December provides the maximum protective impact when schools are fully in session.
