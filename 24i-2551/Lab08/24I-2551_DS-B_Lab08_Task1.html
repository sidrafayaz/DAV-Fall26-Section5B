Task 1: Working Process, Line Explanations & Step 6 Answers

Step 1 (Predict First): In 2007, Norway is the richest country ($49,357), whereas in 1952, Kuwait was the richest by far ($108,382); thus, it is not the same country.
Step 2 (Top 10 Horizontal Bars): Filtered dataset for the active year, sorted descending by gdpPercap, sliced top 10 rows, and drew horizontal SVG bars with countries on the left using d3.scaleLinear and d3.scaleBand.
Step 3 (Colour by Continent): Mapped colors using the Vibrant Modern Palette. Europe, Asia, and the Americas appear in the top 10; Africa is completely absent.
Step 4 (Hover Interaction): Attached mouseover, mousemove, and mouseout listeners to show a floating HTML tooltip with country name, continent, and exact formatted USD GDP per capita.
Step 5 (Transitions): Buttons trigger animated D3 transitions (750ms). Data bound with key function d => d.country ensures smooth sliding and reordering.
Step 6.1 (Which line keeps only the top 10?): The JavaScript array method .slice(0, 10) extracts only the first 10 items from the descending-sorted country array.
Step 6.2 (Why do we write d => d.country inside .data(...)?): Writing d => d.country acts as a key function that gives each SVG rectangle object constancy, allowing D3 to track and animate existing country bars between years instead of incorrectly morphing by array index.
Step 6.3 (In 1952, why do the other bars look so short?): Kuwait's GDP per capita was unusually massive ($108,382 due to its 1950s oil boom), expanding the linear x-axis scale so wide that the second richest country ($14,734) occupies less than 15% of the chart width.
